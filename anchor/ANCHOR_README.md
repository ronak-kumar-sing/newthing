## The Vision

Every student faces the same invisible enemy — not lack of talent, not lack of resources, but **lack of awareness**. You don't know how much time you spend gaming. You don't know how many days are left until your life changes forever. You don't know if yesterday was better than today. And you definitely don't know what happened in those 300 unread WhatsApp messages.

**Anchor** is a single unified dashboard — a Personal Student Life OS — that sits on your desktop every single day and answers four fundamental questions before you even start:

- **Where did my time go?**
- **How much time do I have left?**
- **Am I progressing?**
- **What is happening around me?**

It is not a productivity app. It is not a habit tracker. It is not a to-do list. It is the **cockpit of your student life** — one place that combines self-awareness, discipline, intelligence, and daily rhythm into a single, honest screen.

## The Problem

### 1. Screen Time and Gaming Addiction

The problem is not that students play games. The problem is that **they don't know they spent 4 hours doing it**. There is no mirror. No honest number staring back at you. Apps that track screen time exist on phones, but when a student sits at a desktop or laptop — which is where most studying and most gaming happens — there is nothing. No widget on the corner of the screen gently reminding you that you've been on YouTube for 2 hours and 14 minutes when you were supposed to be studying for your exam.

The moment you make this number visible — always on screen, not buried inside a settings menu — behavior changes. Not because of guilt. Because of **awareness**. You cannot fix what you cannot see.

### 2. College Notification Chaos — WhatsApp 100+ Unread

Every Indian college student lives in 5 to 15 WhatsApp groups. Department group. Class group. Lab group. Project group. Club group. Seniors group. Each one floods with 50 to 200 messages every day. Inside that noise are exactly 3 things that actually matter: a deadline, a notice about a placement drive, or a schedule change. But to find those 3 things, you either spend 30 minutes scrolling through memes and forwards — or you ignore everything and miss the deadline entirely.

There is no middle ground right now. You either waste time or you miss things. **Anchor eliminates this tradeoff** by doing the reading for you and surfacing only what matters.

### 3. Discipline and Motivation Collapse

Motivation is not the problem. Every student has felt motivated — on the first day of a semester, after watching a motivational video, after failing a test. The real problem is that **motivation has no structure to sit on**. It comes and goes. What replaces it is discipline — and discipline requires a system, a routine, and daily accountability.

Without a system that checks in with you every single day, motivation is just a feeling that evaporates by noon. Students need something that holds them accountable not through punishment but through **consistent, honest visibility** into their own behavior.

### 4. Not Sticking to One Thing

The most common pattern among distracted students is starting strong and abandoning quickly. A new study schedule lasts three days. A new habit lasts a week. A goal set on January 1 is forgotten by February. The root cause is not laziness — it is the absence of a **streak, a commitment record, and a cost to breaking it**.

When nothing tracks your consistency, there is nothing at stake when you break it. Anchor makes consistency visible and makes breaking it feel real — not punishing, but honest.

### 5. No Awareness of Time Remaining

Students in their final year often say the same thing: *"I thought I had more time."* The truth is that time to graduation, time to placement season, time to becoming financially independent — these are all finite and countable numbers. But nobody looks at the number. Nobody converts "2 semesters left" into "180 days left to build the skills that define my career."

When you can see the number shrinking every single day, it stops being abstract. It becomes **real and urgent without being stressful** — because it is paired with a plan and progress, not just a countdown.

### 6. No Sense of Progress in Life

The hardest question a student can ask themselves is: *"Am I actually getting better?"* Not in one subject. Not at one skill. In life — as a person, as a professional, as someone building toward something. There is no dashboard for this. There is no graph that shows whether this week was better than last week across all the things that matter to you.

Without this, students often feel stuck even when they are moving. Or feel fine even when they are slipping. **Anchor makes your life measurable** — not to reduce you to numbers, but to give you an honest reflection of the direction you are heading.

## What is Anchor?

Anchor is a personal life operating system that lives on your computer as a desktop application. It opens every morning with a single unified screen — your daily cockpit. Everything you need to be aware of, everything you need to do, and everything you need to stay disciplined is on one screen.

It has no notifications to ignore. It has no premium upsells. It has no social features. It is **entirely private, entirely personal, and entirely yours**.

The name "Anchor" reflects exactly what it does: in a sea of distractions, gaming sessions, group chats, and endless to-do lists that go nowhere — Anchor is what keeps you grounded. It is the fixed point in your day that everything else rotates around.

## Core Modules

---

### Module 1: Screen Mirror Widget

**What it does:**
A persistent, always-visible widget that sits in a corner of your screen and displays your real-time screen time for the day. It tracks total active time on your computer, broken down by category — productive (documents, study apps, coding), neutral (YouTube, music, browsing), and distracted (games, social media, streaming).

**What it looks like:**
A compact strip at the top or side of your screen. Always visible. Never blocking. It shows today's total screen time, the current session length, and a simple color indicator — green if your productive-to-distracted ratio is healthy, amber if it is borderline, and a quiet red if you have significantly overshot your own limit.

**Why it matters:**
You cannot close it by accident. You cannot minimize it and forget. It is always there — a mirror, not a judge. Over time, just seeing the number changes how long you stay in a gaming session, because the number makes the cost real. This is behavioral design at its simplest: **make the invisible visible**.

**Key behaviors:**
- Resets every morning at a time you choose
- Lets you set a daily "distraction limit" — you choose the number, Anchor only shows you whether you are within it
- Weekly summary every Sunday showing your 7-day trend
- The widget remembers which apps you label as productive vs distracted, and learns your pattern over time

---

### Module 2: The Independence Clock

**What it does:**
A permanent countdown on your dashboard that shows exactly how many days, hours, and minutes remain until a goal date you define. This could be your graduation date, the day your placement season begins, the day you plan to start earning, or any date that represents your version of "independence."

**What it looks like:**
A large, calm counter — not alarming, not red, not urgent. Just honest. Below the counter is a small "daily target" bar: if there are 200 days left and you have 200 things to accomplish, Anchor quietly shows you how many of those things you have done so far and whether you are on pace.

**Why it matters:**
Abstract timelines feel infinite. "I have two semesters left" does not create urgency. "I have 147 days left" does — especially when paired with what you are doing with those days. The clock does not exist to create panic. It exists to make **the finite nature of this phase of your life visible every single day**, so that the decision to spend 4 hours gaming is made with that number in full view.

**Key behaviors:**
- You set the goal date and what it represents to you — Anchor never judges the goal
- Optional: connect it to a list of milestones you want to hit before that date
- A small daily quote or reflection appears below the counter — not generic motivation, but something relevant to where you are in the countdown (early, mid, close)
- On milestone days (100 days left, 50 days left, etc.) the screen shows a brief acknowledgment — not a celebration, just a quiet marker

---

### Module 3: WhatsApp Digest

**What it does:**
Once a day — or whenever you trigger it — Anchor reads your WhatsApp groups and produces a clean, scannable digest of everything important that happened since you last checked. Instead of 300 messages, you see 6 to 10 bullet points of what actually matters: announcements, deadlines, events, and action items.

**What it looks like:**
A digest panel in the Anchor dashboard, formatted like a clean newsletter. Each group gets its own section. The bullet points are written in plain, clear language. Anything that sounds like a deadline or an action item is highlighted. Anything that is clearly a forward, a meme, or casual conversation is silently ignored.

**Why it matters:**
This single feature alone saves 30 to 60 minutes of daily scrolling. But more importantly, it means you can stay fully disconnected from WhatsApp for most of the day — which directly improves your focus and reduces the "let me just check quickly" loop that costs you hours every week. You read the digest in 90 seconds. You know everything you need to know. You close it and get back to work.

**Key behaviors:**
- Works on any WhatsApp group you connect — department group, class group, project group, placement group
- Groups are ranked by importance — you decide which groups are "high priority" and their content appears first
- Digest is generated fresh every morning and on demand
- You can star a specific digest item to turn it into a task in the Task Command Center
- The digest keeps a 7-day archive so you can look back at what was announced last week

---

### Module 4: Life Progress Board

**What it does:**
A visual dashboard that shows your progress across every dimension of your life that you choose to track. Not just tasks — but habits, skills, consistency streaks, weekly goals, and an overall "life score" for the week.

**What it looks like:**
A grid of progress bars and charts on your Anchor dashboard. Each bar represents one area you are tracking — for example: study hours this week, exercise sessions this week, coding practice, sleep quality, days without excessive gaming, books in progress. Below the grid is a weekly comparison: this week vs last week, this month vs last month.

**Why it matters:**
Most students track nothing and therefore know nothing. They feel like they are progressing when they are not, and feel stuck when they are actually moving forward. The Life Progress Board replaces feeling with **data about your own life**. You define what matters, Anchor measures it, and you see the truth every day.

**Key behaviors:**
- You define your own "life dimensions" — there are no defaults imposed on you
- Some dimensions are automatic (screen time, tasks completed, WhatsApp digest read)
- Others are manual check-ins — you rate your own sleep, energy, or focus for the day with a single tap
- A weekly "reflection prompt" appears every Sunday — one honest question about the week, your answer saved privately
- A 30-day trend line shows where you are improving and where you are slipping
- "Streaks" are shown for any dimension where consistency matters — studying every day, exercising, avoiding gaming past midnight

---

### Module 5: AI Daily Coach

**What it does:**
An AI assistant built directly into Anchor that understands your full context — your progress, your countdown, your task list, your WhatsApp digest, and your recent behavior — and gives you genuinely personalized guidance every day. Not generic advice. Advice based on your actual data.

**What it looks like:**
A conversation panel inside Anchor. Every morning, the AI Coach generates a short briefing tailored to you: "You have 147 days left. This week you studied 8 hours — less than last week's 11. Your gaming sessions averaged 2.3 hours per day. Today's WhatsApp digest has a placement notice for your department." You can then ask it anything — "What should I prioritize today?" or "Help me plan my next 30 days" — and it answers using what it knows about your life, your goals, and your current state.

**Why it matters:**
Generic AI assistants know nothing about you. Anchor's AI Coach knows everything relevant — because it has access to your own Anchor data. This makes every suggestion specific and actionable instead of generic and forgettable. It is the difference between "you should study more" and "you studied 8 hours this week vs your 12-hour target, here is what you can do in the remaining 4 days of the week to close that gap."

**Key behaviors:**
- Runs entirely on your device — your data never leaves your machine
- Morning briefing is generated automatically and delivered on the Anchor home screen
- You can ask open-ended questions: "Am I on track?", "What am I neglecting?", "How has my focus been this month?"
- The coach can generate a weekly plan, a study schedule for an upcoming exam, or a breakdown of how to use your remaining countdown days
- Over time, the coach learns your patterns and references them: "Last time you had an exam coming up, you tended to underestimate preparation time"

### Module 6: Task Command Center

**What it does:**
A deeply integrated task management layer inside Anchor that syncs with Todoist (and eventually other task apps) to bring all your tasks, projects, and deadlines into one place — with smart prioritization based on your countdown, your progress data, and your daily brief.

**What it looks like:**
A clean task panel that shows today's tasks, this week's tasks, and overdue items — pulled from Todoist but displayed inside Anchor's unified interface. Tasks are automatically tagged by type (academic, personal, project, habit) and sorted by urgency relative to your Independence Clock.

**Why it matters:**
The problem with most task apps is that they live in isolation. Your Todoist does not know that you have 147 days until graduation. It does not know that you spent 3 hours gaming yesterday. Anchor's Task Command Center connects your tasks to your real life context — so the daily recommended task list is not just "oldest deadline first" but **"here is what matters most given where you are and how much time you have."**

**Key behaviors:**
- Full two-way sync with Todoist — anything you add in Anchor appears in Todoist and vice versa
- WhatsApp digest items can be converted into tasks with one click
- Morning brief includes the top 3 tasks for the day — chosen by the AI Coach based on urgency and energy
- "Focus mode" locks in one task at a time and starts a Pomodoro timer with screen time tracking for that session
- At end of day, Anchor shows which tasks were completed and adds them to your Life Progress Board automatically

### Module 7: Morning Brief Screen

**What it does:**
Every morning when you open Anchor, the first thing you see is the Morning Brief — a single, clean screen designed to be consumed in under 60 seconds. It contains everything you need to orient your day: your countdown number, today's weather, your top 3 tasks, your WhatsApp digest summary, one news headline from your chosen field, and your AI Coach's one-line message for the day.

**What it looks like:**
A full-screen view — calm, uncluttered, no notifications, no ads. Think of it as your personal newspaper front page. Each section is compact. You read it, you absorb it, you close it and start your day. The whole interaction is designed to take no more than 60 seconds.

**Why it matters:**
Most people start their day by opening Instagram, or checking WhatsApp, or watching something — and immediately spending their first 20 to 30 minutes in a reactive, passive, distracted state. The Morning Brief flips this. Your first 60 seconds are spent with information that is specifically about **your life, your goals, and your day**. This sets the tone for everything that follows.

**Key behaviors:**
- Opens automatically when Anchor launches
- Can be set as a startup application so it is the first thing on your screen every morning
- Weather is shown for your location
- The news headline is sourced from a field you choose — InfoSec, AI, technology, or any other area you care about
- The AI Coach message changes daily based on your current life data — it is never the same twice
- A "Set your intention" prompt lets you type one sentence about what you want today to be — saved to your progress journal

## A Day in the Life

**7:00 AM — You wake up and open your laptop.**

Anchor launches automatically. The Morning Brief fills the screen. It says: "Day 147 of 365. Weather in Ludhiana: 34°C, clear. Today's top tasks: finish InfoSec assignment, review placement prep notes, push code update. WhatsApp digest ready — 1 important notice from your department group. Your screen time yesterday: 9 hours total, 4.5 hours distracted. AI Coach: You are 3 hours behind your weekly study target. Today is the day to close that gap."

You read it in 45 seconds. You know everything. You close the brief and start your day.

**10:30 AM — You finish a study session.**

You check your progress bar for the week. It moved. You are now at 5 hours of studying this week. The goal is 12. You have 4 days left in the week. The math is visible — 1.75 hours per day to hit your target. Doable.

**2:00 PM — You get pulled into a gaming session.**

The screen mirror widget in the corner shows: 1 hour 14 minutes of distracted time today. Your set limit is 2 hours. You decide to stop. Not because an app nagged you. Because you saw the number and made a conscious choice. That is the difference.

**4:00 PM — Your friend messages: "Did you see the WhatsApp group? There's a placement drive notice."**

You open Anchor's WhatsApp digest. It was already there — summarized in 2 bullet points. Company name, eligibility, registration link, deadline. You click the star icon. It becomes a task in your Task Command Center: "Register for placement drive — deadline Thursday." Done.

**10:00 PM — End of day.**

You open Anchor for the last time. The AI Coach shows today's summary: 6.5 hours productive, 2 hours distracted (within your limit), 4 tasks completed, 1 missed. It asks: "What made today harder than expected?" You type your answer in one sentence. It is saved to your journal. The progress bars update. You close the app.

You go to sleep knowing exactly how today went.

## Why Anchor is Different

**1. It is built around your life, not around tasks.**
Every other productivity app is a task manager with extras. Anchor starts from a different question: what does a student actually need to know every single day to make better decisions? The task list is one answer. The countdown is another. The progress board is a third. They work together — that is what makes Anchor different.

**2. It lives on your desktop, not your phone.**
Your phone is where distractions live. Your phone is where WhatsApp lives. Your phone is where social media pulls you in. Anchor deliberately lives on your computer — the place where you are supposed to be productive — so that your primary work environment also becomes your primary accountability environment.

**3. It does not gamify your life.**
There are no coins, no badges, no streaks that make you feel like you are playing a game instead of living your life. Anchor shows you numbers about your actual behavior and leaves the judgment entirely to you. This respects your intelligence. It trusts you to respond to honesty.

**4. It connects things that other apps keep separate.**
Your WhatsApp digest informs your task list. Your task list informs your AI Coach. Your AI Coach informs your Morning Brief. Your Morning Brief informs your day. Nothing is isolated. Everything talks to everything else — and the result is an app that actually understands your full situation, not just one piece of it.

**5. Your data never leaves your machine.**
No cloud sync (unless you want it). No analytics sent to a server. No account required. Your progress data, your journal entries, your life score — all of it stays on your device. This matters, especially for a student who values privacy.

## Data and Intelligence Layer

Every feature in Anchor is powered by two external systems that handle the heavy lifting: **Todoist** stores and manages all task data, and **Gemini AI** provides the intelligence that turns raw data into meaningful insight. This section explains exactly what each system does inside Anchor, why it was chosen, and how they connect.

---

### Todoist — The Task Database

**What Todoist is in the context of Anchor:**

Todoist is the permanent, structured home for every task that moves through Anchor. Think of it not as a separate app you also have to open — but as the invisible backbone that holds all task data safely organized, synchronized, and retrievable at any time. When you add a task in Anchor, it lives in Todoist. When you complete it, Todoist records the completion with a timestamp. When you look at your Life Progress Board and see "12 tasks completed this week," that number came from Todoist.

Anchor uses the **Todoist REST API v1** (`https://api.todoist.com/api/v1`) to read and write all task data in real time.

---

**How tasks are created inside Anchor:**

Every time you create a task — whether by typing it in the Task Command Center, converting a WhatsApp digest item with one click, or asking the AI Coach to build you a study plan — Anchor sends that task to Todoist. The task is stored with:

- A clear title describing what needs to be done
- A due date or deadline (so Anchor knows when to surface it)
- A label that categorizes it — Academic, Personal, Project, or Habit
- A priority level — High, Medium, or Low — determined either by you or suggested by the AI Coach based on your countdown and current week
- A project it belongs to — for example, all InfoSec assignment tasks go under an "Academic" project, daily habit tasks go under a "Daily Routine" project

Anchor creates and maintains these Todoist projects automatically the first time you set it up. You never need to open Todoist separately to organize anything — Anchor manages the structure for you.

---

**How tasks are surfaced to you:**

Every morning, Anchor asks Todoist for everything due today, everything overdue, and everything due in the next 3 days. This becomes your Task Command Center view inside the Morning Brief and the dashboard. The AI Coach then reads this list and picks the top 3 tasks for your day — not just the ones due soonest, but the ones that matter most given your countdown, your energy level, and what you have been neglecting.

Throughout the day, the Task Command Center stays synced. If you complete something on your phone in the real Todoist app, it reflects in Anchor. If you complete something in Anchor, it reflects in Todoist. The data is always the same in both places.

---

**What happens when a task is completed:**

This is where Anchor's relationship with Todoist becomes meaningful for your progress tracking. When you mark a task as done in Anchor, the following sequence happens:

1. Anchor calls the Todoist API's **Close Task** endpoint, which marks the task as completed and records the exact timestamp of completion.
2. The task moves from your active list to Todoist's completion history — permanently stored there with its completion date.
3. Anchor reads this completion event and adds it to your Life Progress Board for today — your "tasks completed" count for the day goes up by one.
4. If the task had a label (Academic, Habit, Project), the completion is counted in that specific category's weekly progress bar.
5. If you completed all tasks for the day, Anchor notes this as a "full day" in your streak tracker.

Todoist keeps a permanent history of every task you have ever completed, with the exact date and time. This is the data source for your weekly report, your monthly report, and the AI Coach's ability to say things like: "You completed 34 tasks last month — the best month you've had."

---

**What Todoist stores on your behalf:**

| Data | Where it lives | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Every task you create | Todoist cloud | Never lost, always retrievable |
| Task labels and priorities | Todoist cloud | Enables category-based progress tracking |
| Due dates and deadlines | Todoist cloud | Powers the Morning Brief and daily task list |
| Completion timestamps | Todoist cloud | Powers the Life Progress Board and weekly report |
| Project structure | Todoist cloud | Keeps tasks organized by area of life |
| Recurring tasks | Todoist cloud | Daily habits auto-reappear every morning |

---

**Recurring tasks and daily habits:**

Habits are a special kind of task in Anchor — things like "review flashcards," "30 minutes of exercise," or "read for 20 minutes." These are created in Todoist as recurring tasks with a daily schedule. Every morning they automatically reappear in your task list. When you complete them, they are marked done for that day and reappear the next morning fresh. Anchor tracks your streak — how many consecutive days you completed each habit — using Todoist's completion history as the source of truth.

---

**Reminders and alerts:**

Todoist has a built-in reminder system that Anchor uses to send you desktop notifications. When a high-priority task is due within 2 hours and you have not started it yet, Anchor triggers a Todoist reminder that appears as a gentle desktop notification — not a flood of alerts, just a single honest nudge at the right time.

### Gemini AI — The Intelligence Engine

**What Gemini AI is in the context of Anchor:**

Gemini is Google's AI model, accessed through the Gemini API. In Anchor, Gemini is the intelligence layer — the system that reads your data, understands your context, and produces personalized output that feels relevant to your actual life rather than generic advice. Every piece of language that Anchor generates — the morning brief text, the AI Coach's daily message, the WhatsApp digest bullet points, the weekly plan — is produced by Gemini.

Anchor sends Gemini carefully constructed context — your countdown number, your task list, your screen time summary, your week's progress — and asks it to respond in a specific, structured way. The result is output that feels like it was written by someone who actually knows your situation.

---

**What Gemini powers inside each Anchor module:**

**Morning Brief text generation:**
Every morning, Anchor gathers all your data for the day — weather, schedule, top tasks, countdown, screen time from yesterday, AI Coach message — and sends it to Gemini with a prompt that asks it to write the coach's one-line message for the day. This message is never a template or a random quote. It is written by Gemini based on what is actually happening in your life that day. "You have 3 overdue tasks and a 4-hour gaming session yesterday. Today is a reset day — start with the smallest task and build momentum" is the kind of message Gemini produces because it has the actual data to reason from.

**WhatsApp Digest summarization:**
When Anchor reads your WhatsApp group messages, it sends the raw message log to Gemini with a prompt asking it to: identify important announcements, extract deadlines and action items, dismiss obvious noise (forwards, memes, casual replies), and format the output as clean, numbered bullet points. Gemini reads 300 messages and returns 5 to 8 bullet points in under 5 seconds. It also identifies which bullet points are action items — "Register by Thursday" — so Anchor can offer to convert them into tasks automatically.

**AI Daily Coach responses:**
When you ask the AI Coach a question — "Am I on track this week?", "Help me plan for my exam next Friday", "What am I neglecting?" — Anchor bundles your question together with your full current context (task list, progress board data, countdown, recent screen time, completed habits this week) and sends it to Gemini. Gemini reads all of this and gives you an answer that is specific to your situation. It does not say "you should study more." It says "you have studied 6 of your 12-hour target this week — there are 3 days left, which means 2 hours per day will get you there. Your exam prep project has 4 tasks remaining, all marked high priority. I would start with the shortest one today to build momentum."

**Weekly plan generation:**
When you ask the AI Coach to generate a plan for the upcoming week, Anchor sends Gemini your entire task backlog from Todoist, your progress from the past week, your countdown number, and your daily availability. Gemini then distributes your tasks across the week — balancing workload, grouping related tasks, protecting your morning hours for deep work — and returns a day-by-day plan that Anchor displays in the Task Command Center and saves back to Todoist as due dates.

**Progress pattern analysis:**
Every Sunday, Anchor bundles your full week's data — tasks completed by category, screen time breakdown, habits maintained, days where you hit your study target — and asks Gemini to write a weekly reflection. This is a 3 to 4 sentence honest summary of what the week looked like, what the strongest pattern was, and one specific thing to pay attention to next week. It is written in plain, direct language — not cheerful, not discouraging, just honest.

**Placement and deadline awareness:**
When a WhatsApp digest or a task contains something that looks like a placement opportunity — a company name, an eligibility criterion, a registration deadline — Anchor flags it to Gemini, which verifies whether it looks like an actionable opportunity and extracts the key details (company, role type, deadline, eligibility). This saves you from missing placement drives because they were buried in casual group chat conversation.

---

**What Gemini is given vs what it decides:**

Gemini does not have direct access to your data. It receives only what Anchor explicitly sends it for each specific request. This means:

| Anchor sends Gemini | Gemini decides |
|---|---|
| Your countdown number | What tone to use in the coach message |
| Today's task list (from Todoist) | Which tasks to prioritize today |
| This week's progress numbers | What to highlight in the weekly reflection |
| Raw WhatsApp message log | What is important vs what is noise |
| Your question to the AI Coach | How to answer it using the provided context |
| Your screen time from yesterday | Whether to acknowledge it in the morning brief |

Gemini is the writer and reasoner. Anchor is the data provider and the display layer. Your data never permanently lives in Gemini — it is sent per request, used to generate a response, and that is all.

### How They Work Together

The most powerful behavior in Anchor happens when Todoist and Gemini are used in combination. Here are three examples of how they connect:

**Example 1 — Converting a digest into a tracked task:**
The WhatsApp Digest (Gemini) surfaces an announcement: "Placement drive for TCS — register by Thursday, CSE and IS students eligible." You tap the star icon. Anchor creates a task in Todoist: "Register for TCS placement drive" with a due date of Wednesday (one day before the deadline, for safety), labeled "Placement," priority High. It now appears in your Morning Brief, your task list, and it will trigger a reminder on Wednesday morning. One tap turned 200 messages into an action item that will not be forgotten.

**Example 2 — AI Coach reads your week and talks to Todoist:**
You ask the AI Coach: "Build me a study plan for my InfoSec exam on Friday." Gemini receives your question, your Todoist task backlog (all InfoSec-related tasks), the current date, and the countdown to Friday. It generates a day-by-day plan. Anchor then writes the due dates back to Todoist — each task gets the exact day Gemini assigned it. Your study plan is now inside Todoist, showing up in the right order across the week's Morning Briefs, with reminders active.

**Example 3 — End-of-week report:**
Every Sunday evening, Anchor pulls the full week's completed tasks from Todoist — with completion timestamps — and sends them to Gemini along with the screen time summary, habit streak data, and your countdown progress. Gemini writes the weekly reflection. Anchor displays it as a full-screen summary and saves it to your journal. The journal is now a permanent record of your weeks — queryable later ("What was I doing in March?" or "When did I first hit my 12-hour study target?").

**The guiding principle of this layer:**

Todoist remembers everything. Gemini understands everything. Anchor shows you everything. Together, they replace the need to carry your entire life in your head — which is where anxiety, missed deadlines, and lost focus actually come from.

## Phased Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Foundation *(First 4 Weeks)*

The goal of Phase 1 is to build the core screen that you use every single day. Everything in this phase must feel polished and reliable before moving to Phase 2.

- **Morning Brief Screen** — the first thing you see every day
- **Independence Clock** — the countdown with your chosen goal date
- **Basic Screen Time Tracker** — total time, session time, persistent widget
- **Task Command Center (Todoist sync)** — your tasks inside Anchor
- **Daily check-in** — rate your day in 3 taps, saved to your journal

*Success metric for Phase 1: You open Anchor every single morning without being reminded to.*

---

### Phase 2 — Intelligence *(Months 2 and 3)*

The goal of Phase 2 is to make Anchor smarter — it should start to understand your patterns and give you genuinely useful feedback.

- **WhatsApp Digest** — daily AI-powered group chat summary
- **Life Progress Board** — full multi-dimension progress tracking with charts
- **AI Daily Coach** — personalized daily briefing and Q&A based on your data
- **Screen time categorization** — productive vs distracted, with your own labels
- **Weekly reflection** — Sunday summary with trend analysis and one honest question

*Success metric for Phase 2: The AI Coach tells you something about your week that surprises you — and it is true.*

---

### Phase 3 — Independence *(Months 4 to 6)*

The goal of Phase 3 is to make Anchor feel like it genuinely knows you — and to make it useful not just daily but strategically.

- **30-day and 90-day goal planning** — connected to your countdown
- **Focus Mode** — lock into one task, track exactly how long you worked on it, block distractions
- **Placement Tracker** — a dedicated module for tracking applications, interviews, and preparation milestones
- **Monthly report** — a full-page PDF summary of the month: study hours, screen time, progress across all dimensions, tasks completed vs missed
- **Mood and energy journaling** — daily one-word or one-sentence log, visualized as a timeline

*Success metric for Phase 3: You look at your monthly report and feel a genuine sense of accountability to yourself — not shame, not pride, just clarity.*

---

### Phase 4 — Beyond Graduation *(6 Months+)*

Once the student version is working for you, Anchor becomes something bigger:

- **Career Mode** — replace "graduation countdown" with "first job promotion timeline" or "freelance income goal"
- **Skill tracking** — connect certifications, courses, and portfolio items to your independence goal
- **Peer accountability** — optional feature to share only your "life score" (not your data) with one or two trusted friends
- **Cross-device** — everything that was desktop-first becomes available on your phone as a companion view, not a replacement

## The Bigger Picture

Two years from now, if Anchor has worked as intended, here is what changes:

**For you personally:** You graduate with a full record of your final 2 years as a student. Every week, every score, every streak, every journal entry. You know exactly how you spent your time, what habits you built, and what you are proud of. You have a clarity about yourself that most people spend years trying to find.

**As a product:** Anchor started as a tool you built for yourself — but every problem it solves is one that every Indian college student shares. Screen time addiction, WhatsApp chaos, no sense of progress, no discipline system. These are not your problems alone. They are universal. A polished version of Anchor could be shared with classmates, then college groups, then communities of students preparing for placements, then anyone navigating a major life transition.

**The principle at the center:** Most technology is designed to take your attention. Anchor is designed to **return it to you**. That is a rare thing to build — and a rare thing to use.
